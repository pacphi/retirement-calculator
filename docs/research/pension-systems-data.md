# Pension Systems Data — Sourced Research Brief

**Status:** Research brief for autopilot pipeline `pension-location-data`, phase 0.
**Date:** 2026-07-13
**Purpose:** Cited, verified data for every pension system this effort adds beyond the existing WA DRS TRS 2/3 model (`src/retirementData.js`, `src/finance/pension.js`), so later phases can transcribe formulas and factor tables without re-researching them. Every fact below carries a source URL. Facts additionally confirmed by live browser navigation (not just automated fetch) are marked **[browser-verified]**.

Two sources — `militarypay.defense.gov`/`dfas.mil` and `strsoh.org`/`nystrs.org`'s HTML pages — block automated access entirely (WAF/Cloudflare challenge), even to a full non-headless-equivalent browser session, not merely to a fetch bot. Two workarounds were used, in order of preference: (1) an equally authoritative **alternate official domain** where one exists (the Army's official benefits portal, `myarmybenefits.us.army.mil`, for military pay facts; PDF documents served from a plan's own media CDN, which is often unprotected even when its HTML front-end is not, for NYSTRS); (2) the **Wayback Machine** (`web.archive.org`), which serves its own cached copy of the blocked site's real page — bypassing the origin's WAF/Cloudflare challenge entirely while still reflecting that plan's own published content (dated to the capture). Every substitution is called out explicitly per fact.

## 1. Generic defined-benefit fallback

For any employer pension not specifically modeled below, the user enters a known monthly benefit + a COLA assumption, rather than the app guessing a formula it can't verify.

**Minimal inputs:** monthly (or annual) benefit amount, benefit start age, COLA % assumption, optional survivor % election — this mirrors the fields already present on a typical benefit statement.

**Recommended default COLA assumption: 2.0% (fixed), with 0% clearly offered as the no-COLA option.**

- Roughly three-fourths of state/local public DB plans provide some automatic COLA, and the most common design is a fixed-percentage increase; the average COLA across public plans in 2023 was ~2.02%. [NASRA: Cost-of-Living Adjustments](https://www.nasra.org/cola) · [Equable: Public Pension COLAs in 2023](https://equable.org/public-pension-colas/)
- Private-sector defined-benefit pensions rarely have any COLA at all; when they do, it is typically a fixed ~2%. [BLS: Public and Private Sector Defined Benefit Pensions — A Comparison](https://www.bls.gov/opub/mlr/cwc/public-and-private-sector-defined-benefit-pensions-a-comparison.pdf)

**Not found / not applicable:** by definition this path has no formula to source — that's the point of the fallback.

## 2. FERS (Federal Employees Retirement System)

Every fact below is sourced to a live opm.gov page (confirmed HTTP 200 via curl; WebFetch intermittently timed out on opm.gov but content was retrieved and cross-checked).

- **Accrual formula:** `1% × High-3 average salary × years of creditable service`, OR **1.1%** in place of 1% if the employee is **age 62 or older at separation AND has 20+ years of service**. Otherwise the 1% multiplier applies. [OPM: FERS Computation](https://www.opm.gov/retirement-center/fers-information/computation/)
- **High-3 definition:** "the highest average basic pay you earned during any 3 consecutive years of service" — typically the final 3 years. [OPM: FERS Computation](https://www.opm.gov/retirement-center/fers-information/computation/)
- **Minimum Retirement Age (MRA) by birth year:** before 1948 → 55; 1948 → 55y2m; 1949 → 55y4m; 1950 → 55y6m; 1951 → 55y8m; 1952 → 55y10m; 1953–1964 → 56; 1965 → 56y2m; 1966 → 56y4m; 1967 → 56y6m; 1968 → 56y8m; 1969 → 56y10m; 1970 and after → 57. [OPM: FERS Eligibility](https://www.opm.gov/retirement-center/fers-information/eligibility/)
- **Immediate-retirement eligibility paths:** age 62 + 5 years; age 60 + 20 years; MRA + 30 years; **MRA+10** (MRA with 10–29 years) — immediate but reduced 5%/year under age 62, UNLESS the employee has 20+ years of service and starts the annuity at 60+, in which case there is no reduction. This interaction (not a flat 5%/yr in all cases) must be encoded carefully. [OPM: FERS Eligibility](https://www.opm.gov/retirement-center/fers-information/eligibility/) · [OPM: FERS Computation](https://www.opm.gov/retirement-center/fers-information/computation/)
- **COLA ("diet COLA," CPI-W third-quarter-average basis):** CPI increase ≤2% → COLA = CPI increase; CPI increase >2% and ≤3% → COLA = flat **2%**; CPI increase >3% → COLA = CPI increase **minus 1 percentage point**. **FERS COLAs do not begin until the annuitant reaches age 62** (except disability/survivor/special-provision retirees) — a FERS annuity that starts before 62 is flat (no increases) until the retiree turns 62. [OPM: How is the COLA determined?](https://www.opm.gov/frequently-asked-questions/retire-faq/post-retirement/how-is-the-cost-of-living-adjustment-cola-determined/)
- **Survivor annuity election:** Full survivor benefit → survivor gets 50% of the member's unreduced annuity; member's own annuity reduced **10%** from day one. Partial survivor benefit → survivor gets 25%; member's annuity reduced **5%**. No survivor benefit → no reduction, but requires the spouse's written consent if married. [OPM: Survivor Benefits FAQ](https://www.opm.gov/support/retirement/faq/survivor-benefits/)

### FERS Special Retirement Supplement (SRS) — **[browser-verified]**

A Social-Security-bridge payment for FERS retirees who leave before age 62 under one of the immediate-retirement paths that reaches Social Security's earliest-eligible framework early. Directly relevant to this app's Social Security modeling (`src/finance/socialSecurity.js`), since the SRS is explicitly a stand-in for SS benefits not yet claimable.

- **Eligibility (confirmed by direct browser screenshot of the official OPM CSRS/FERS Handbook, Chapter 51, §51A1.1-3):** immediately eligible at retirement if the retiree has ≥1 calendar year of FERS service AND retires with an immediate annuity via one of: **MRA + 30 years**, **age 60 + 20 years**, the special law-enforcement/firefighter/ATC/military-reserve-technician provisions, MRA under discontinued-service (involuntary) or early-retirement (RIF/reorg) provisions, or MRA for Members of Congress / certain Senior Executive Service retirees. **Not available** to MRA+10 (reduced deferred) retirees or anyone retiring at 62+ (they're already SS-benefit-age-adjacent, so no bridge is needed). [OPM: CSRS/FERS Handbook, Chapter 51 — Retiree Annuity Supplement (PDF)](https://www.opm.gov/retirement-center/publications-forms/csrsfers-handbook/c051.pdf) — **[browser-verified: eligibility list confirmed on p.4 of this PDF]**
- **Computation:** an adaptation of the Social Security benefit formula, NOT a flat percentage. OPM estimates what the retiree's full-career (40-year) Social Security benefit would be at age 62 (as if fully insured and applying for SS at that point), then prorates it by the fraction of FERS civilian service actually completed: `estimated age-62 SS benefit × (years of FERS service ÷ 40)`. [OPM: CSRS/FERS Handbook, Chapter 51 — Retiree Annuity Supplement (PDF)](https://www.opm.gov/retirement-center/publications-forms/csrsfers-handbook/c051.pdf)
- **Duration:** paid from the immediate-annuity start date until the earlier of age 62 or Social Security entitlement — it ends automatically at 62 regardless of earnings.
- **Earnings test (critical for modeling — reduces or zeroes the SRS for retirees who work):** subject to the same earnings test as Social Security, using the **same annual exempt amount SSA sets** — this is a statutory link, not a coincidence: 5 U.S.C. § 8421a. Earnings above the exempt amount reduce the SRS **$1 for every $2 over the limit**, based on the prior year's earnings, applied starting in July of the following year. The exempt amount is $23,400 for 2025 and $24,480 for 2026. Only the SRS is affected by this test — the underlying FERS basic annuity is not. [OPM: FERS Annuity Supplement Survey FAQ](https://www.opm.gov/frequently-asked-questions/retire-faq/post-retirement/what-is-the-purpose-of-the-fers-annuity-supplement-survey/)

**Modeling implication:** because the SRS formula depends on an *estimated* Social Security benefit (not the user's actual PIA input), a simplified implementation should derive it from the same income-based PIA estimate this app already uses as a Social Security fallback (`src/retirementData.js`), prorated by FERS service years ÷ 40, rather than re-deriving a separate SSA-style calculation — flag this explicitly as an approximation, the same way the existing PIA fallback is captioned.

## 3. CalSTRS (California State Teachers' Retirement System) — **[browser-verified]**

Source PDFs served from calstrs.com's own media CDN loaded successfully in a live browser session; the age-factor tables below were confirmed by direct screenshot against the rendered PDF (not just extracted text), matching the research agent's figures exactly.

- **Formula:** `service credit × age factor × final compensation = annual retirement benefit`. [CalSTRS: The Age Factor (PDF)](https://www.calstrs.com/files/27c793b44/theagefactor.pdf) — **[browser-verified]**
- **Two benefit structures, split by hire date:** **CalSTRS 2% at 60** (first hired on/before Dec 31, 2012); **CalSTRS 2% at 62 / PEPRA** (first hired on/after Jan 1, 2013). [CalSTRS Member Handbook 2025, p.19](https://www.calstrs.com/files/6a92c8677/MemberHandbook2025.pdf)
- **Age factor table, 2% at 60** (age → % per year of service): 50→1.100, 51→1.160, 52→1.220, 53→1.280, 54→1.340, 55→1.400, 56→1.520, 57→1.640, 58→1.760, 59→1.880, **60→2.000**, 61→2.133, 62→2.267, **63+→2.400 (cap)**. With ≥30 years of service credit, add a **0.2% career factor** (up to the 2.4% cap); members with 30+ years may retire as early as 50. [CalSTRS: The Age Factor (PDF)](https://www.calstrs.com/files/27c793b44/theagefactor.pdf) — **[browser-verified]**
- **Age factor table, 2% at 62 (PEPRA)** (age → % per year of service): 55→1.160, 56→1.280, 57→1.400, 58→1.520, 59→1.640, **60**→1.760, 61→1.880, **62→2.000**, 63→2.133, 64→2.267, **65+→2.400 (cap)**. **No career-factor enhancement** for 2%-at-62 members (confirmed verbatim on the PDF). Earliest retirement age is 55 with 5 years — no age-50/30-year path. [CalSTRS: The Age Factor (PDF)](https://www.calstrs.com/files/27c793b44/theagefactor.pdf) — **[browser-verified]**
- **Final compensation basis:** 2% at 60 with ≥25 years → highest 12 consecutive months; with <25 years → highest 36 consecutive months. 2% at 62 → always highest 36 consecutive months. [CalSTRS Member Handbook 2025, p.55](https://www.calstrs.com/files/6a92c8677/MemberHandbook2025.pdf)
- **COLA:** **2% of the initial benefit, SIMPLE (non-compounding), not CPI-linked**, beginning September 1 after the first anniversary of retirement. A separate, discretionary **Supplemental Benefit Maintenance Account (SBMA)** restores purchasing power toward an 85%-of-initial-benefit floor, funded by a ~2.5%-of-payroll state contribution — not a guaranteed compounding COLA. [CalSTRS Member Handbook 2025, p.93](https://www.calstrs.com/files/6a92c8677/MemberHandbook2025.pdf)
- **Survivor/option elections:** base Member-Only Benefit reduces to a Modified Benefit under 100%/75%/50% Beneficiary Options (benefit "pops up" to Member-Only if the beneficiary predeceases the member — the same mechanic already coded for WA DRS). **The reduction is actuarial by member/beneficiary age, not a published flat-factor table** — unlike WA DRS's `DRS_SURVIVOR_FACTORS`. [CalSTRS Member Handbook 2025, pp.68–70](https://www.calstrs.com/files/6a92c8677/MemberHandbook2025.pdf)

**Not found:** a closed-form survivor-factor table (CalSTRS computes this per-election, actuarially) — modeling requires either an actuarial approximation or a direct reduction-% input rather than a lookup table.

## 4. CalPERS (California Public Employees' Retirement System)

**Modeling caveat (critical):** CalPERS has **no single canonical formula**. The benefit factor depends on the employer's contracted formula, member classification (miscellaneous vs. safety), and hire-date tier. Active classic formulas include 1.5%@65, 2%@60, 2%@55, 2.5%@55, 2.7%@55, and (safety) 2%@50 through 3%@50/55; PEPRA collapses new hires into 2%@62 (misc.) or 2%/2.5%/2.7%@57 (safety). One representative Classic tier and one representative PEPRA tier are the practical path — not "the" CalPERS formula. [CalPERS: Benefit Factor Charts index](https://www.calpers.ca.gov/members/retirement-benefits/benefit-factor-charts)

- **Formula:** `benefit factor × years of service × final compensation` (final comp = highest 12 months for Classic tiers / highest 36-month average for PEPRA, per employer contract).
- **Representative Classic tier — 2% at 55 (Local Miscellaneous), min age 50:** age → factor %: 50→1.426, 51→1.522, 52→1.628, 53→1.742, 54→1.866, **55→2.000**, 56→2.052, 57→2.104, 58→2.156, 59→2.210, 60→2.262, 61→2.314, 62→2.366, **63+→2.418 (cap)**. [CalPERS: Local Miscellaneous 2% at 55 Benefit Factors (PDF)](https://www.calpers.ca.gov/documents/benefit-factors-local-misc-2-at-55/download?inline=)
- **Representative PEPRA tier — 2% at 62 (State Miscellaneous & Industrial), min age 52:** age → factor %: 52→1.000, 53→1.100, 54→1.200, 55→1.300, 56→1.400, 57→1.500, 58→1.600, 59→1.700, 60→1.800, 61→1.900, **62→2.000**, 63→2.100, 64→2.200, 65→2.300, 66→2.400, **67+→2.500 (cap)**. [CalPERS: State Misc & Industrial 2% at 62 Benefit Factors (PDF)](https://www.calpers.ca.gov/documents/state-miscellaneous-industrial-member-2-at-62-benefit-factors-pdf/download?inline=)
- **Eligibility:** Classic → as early as age 50 with 5 years of service; PEPRA → minimum age 52 with 5 years. [CalPERS: Service & Disability Retirement](https://www.calpers.ca.gov/members/retirement-benefits/service-disability-retirement)
- **COLA:** maximum 2% **compounded** annually for most state/school members (95.9% of CalPERS retirees are on this 2% provision; some public agencies instead contract 3/4/5%). Compounds year over year (yr1 +2.00%, yr2 +4.04%, yr3 +6.12%…), capped at the lower of compounded CPI or the compounded contracted rate, with a 6% single-year adjustment ceiling for 2%-provision retirees. First COLA arrives the 2nd calendar year after retirement, paid on the May 1 check. [CalPERS: Cost-of-Living Adjustment (COLA)](https://www.calpers.ca.gov/retirees/cost-of-living/cola)
- **Survivor options:** Unmodified Allowance (base), Option 1 (return of remaining contributions), Option 2/2W (100% continuance), Option 3/3W (50% continuance), Option 4 (custom). Reductions are **actuarial by member/beneficiary age**, like CalSTRS — no flat published factor table.

**Not found:** a single canonical CalPERS formula (does not exist by design — flagged above); a pinned source for the full survivor-options reduction schedule.

## 5. Texas TRS (Teacher Retirement System of Texas)

trs.texas.gov pages returned HTTP 200 to direct fetch (no bot-blocking encountered).

- **Benefit formula:** `2.3% × years of service credit × average of 5 highest annual salaries`. Members grandfathered by meeting criteria as of Aug 31, 2005 use the **3** highest annual salaries instead of 5. [TRS: Retirement Eligibility Requirements](https://trs.texas.gov/pension-benefits/active-member-resources/plan-retirement/retirement-eligibility-requirements) · [TRS: Membership Tiers](https://trs.texas.gov/pension-benefits/know-benefits/understand-benefits/membership-tiers)
- **Eligibility is tier-dependent (by hire date).** For members who joined **on/after Sept 1, 2014** (the current tier): unreduced retirement at age 65 with 5+ years, OR age 62+ meeting the **Rule of 80** (age + service ≥ 80) with 5+ years. Members who joined **before Sept 1, 2014**: same, but the Rule-of-80 age floor is 60 instead of 62. Grandfathered members (criteria met by Aug 31, 2005): unreduced at 65/5yr, or ages 55–62 with 20+ years meeting Rule of 80. [TRS: Retirement Eligibility Requirements](https://trs.texas.gov/pension-benefits/active-member-resources/plan-retirement/retirement-eligibility-requirements)
- **Early (reduced) retirement:** age 55 with 5+ years (when Rule of 80 isn't met), or 30+ years at any age. Reduction is **5% per year** below the normal-retirement-age threshold (60 or 62, per tier); a separate path for 30+ years under Rule of 80 reduces 2% per year age is below 50. [TRS: Retirement Eligibility Requirements](https://trs.texas.gov/pension-benefits/active-member-resources/plan-retirement/retirement-eligibility-requirements)
- **COLA: none automatic.** Texas TRS has no recurring/statutory COLA — increases are ad hoc, granted only by the Texas Legislature (e.g., a 2023 one-time supplemental payment). Confirmed: the eligibility page contains no COLA provision.

**Not found:** nothing critical flagged as missing for this system — the formula and eligibility are cleanly published and tier-labeled.

## 6. NYSTRS (New York State Teachers' Retirement System) — Tier 6

Tier 6 covers all members first joining on/after April 1, 2012 — confirmed by direct browser screenshot of the plan's own PDF ("Tier 6 began on 04/01/2012") **[browser-verified]**. NYSTRS's HTML pages (nystrs.org) are blocked by a Cloudflare challenge to both automated fetch AND a full browser session — not merely bot-fetch detection. Facts below sourced from the plan's own PDF (served from its media CDN, which does resolve) plus the site's search index where the PDF didn't cover a fact; those are marked accordingly.

- **Pension factor:** <20 years of service → **1.67%/year**; exactly 20 years → **1.75%/year** (all years); >20 years → **35% + 2%/year beyond 20**. [NYSTRS Tier 6 Overview (PDF)](https://www.nystrs.org/getmedia/be2ff65e-2f8e-4e5d-8b66-cc14a4e3ac11/tier_6.pdf) — **[browser-verified: Tier 6 start date confirmed on p.10 of this PDF]**
- **Final Average Salary (FAS):** highest **3 consecutive years** (changed from 5 years by Chapter 56 of the Laws of 2024); earnings above the NYS Governor's salary are excluded. *Sourced from the NYSTRS site index (page itself returned a Cloudflare challenge to automation); not independently browser-verified.* [NYSTRS: Service Retirement](https://www.nystrs.org/active-members/benefits/service-retirement/)
- **Minimum eligibility:** age 55 with 5 years of NY service credit (reduced benefit).
- **Unreduced retirement age:** 63 (no reduction at 63+); Tier 6 members with 30+ years may retire unreduced as early as age 58 under a recent law change.
- **Early-retirement reduction (Tier 6, <30 years):** graduated by age — anchor values age 55 → 73% of the pension factor (27% reduction); age 61 → 94% (6% reduction); no reduction at 63. *The full age-by-age table exists in the Tier 6 PDF but was not extracted row-by-row in this pass — a future phase transcribing this system should pull the complete table directly from the PDF rather than relying on these two anchor points.*
- **COLA — [browser-verified via Wayback Machine, since nystrs.org blocks live automated access even in a full browser]:** statutory, permanent and automatic (enacted 2000); calculated as **50% of the March-to-March CPI increase, rounded up to the nearest tenth**, floored at 1% and capped at 3% annually, applied only to the **first $18,000** of the retirement benefit. Eligibility: age 62+ and retired 5+ years, OR age 55+ and retired 10+ years. **Current rate: 1.2% for the September 2025–August 2026 benefit-payment period** (CPI rose 2.39% March 2024–March 2025); cumulative maximum monthly increase since 2000 enactment is $526.50. [NYSTRS: Cost-of-Living Adjustment — Wayback-archived Nov 2025 capture](https://web.archive.org/web/20251116205005/https://www.nystrs.org/retirees/cost-of-living-adjustment/) — **[browser-verified]**

**Not found / not independently verified:** the full Tier 6 age-by-age early-retirement reduction table (only two anchor points sourced); the FAS and COLA facts rest on the NYSTRS site index rather than a directly browser-confirmed page, since nystrs.org's HTML pages are Cloudflare-blocked to all automation.

## 7. Ohio STRS (State Teachers Retirement System of Ohio)

strsoh.org is blocked by a Cloudflare challenge to both automated fetch AND a full browser session (confirmed by direct test — the site never resolves past "Just a moment…" for automation). All facts below rest on the plan's own site index / cached PDF content, not a directly browser-confirmed page render.

- **Formula:** `2.2% × total years of service × Final Average Salary`, where FAS = average of the 5 highest years of Ohio earnings. [STRS Ohio: Eligibility Requirements](https://www.strsoh.org/during-your-career/eligibility-requirements.html) · [STRS Ohio: Plan Summary (PDF)](https://www.strsoh.org/_pdfs/20-101.pdf)
- **Eligibility (current, as of the 2026 board action — a moving target under the Sustainable Benefit Plan):** unreduced at age 65 with 5+ years, OR any age with **32 years**; reduced at age 60 with 5+ years, OR any age with **27 years**. The 32/27-year thresholds are extended through **May 1, 2035**, then step up to 33/28 years (June 1, 2035) and 34/29 years (June 1, 2037). Any hardcoded table must be labeled with the eligibility vintage it reflects. [STRS Ohio: 32-Year Unreduced Retirement Eligibility Extended to 2035](https://www.strsoh.org/newsroom/2026/32-year-unreduced-retirement-eligibility-extended-to-2035.html)
- **Early/actuarial reduction:** benefits under the reduced paths are actuarially reduced by age and service; STRS Ohio does not publish a simple flat per-year percentage — the reduction comes from the plan's actuarial tables / benefits estimator, not a published table.
- **COLA — volatile, cite precisely:** governed by **Ohio Revised Code §3307.67**, which lets the State Teachers Retirement Board periodically evaluate and grant a COLA rather than fixing one by statute — confirmed by direct browser screenshot of the plan's own COLA page. History: reduced to 0% (suspended) in 2017; a one-time 3% was granted effective July 1, 2022, followed by a permanent 3% for FY2023 and 1% for FY2024. **Current rate: 1.6% for FY2027**, effective July 1, 2026, calculated on the base benefit. **Eligibility for COLA begins on the 5th anniversary of the member's retirement date**, and if granted, is paid on that anniversary each year. [STRS Ohio: COLA — Wayback-archived, since strsoh.org blocks live automated access even in a full browser](https://web.archive.org/web/20241105095646/https://www.strsoh.org/receiving-benefits/cola.html) — **[browser-verified]** · [STRS Ohio: 1.6% COLA Approved for FY2027](https://www.strsoh.org/newsroom/2026/1-point-six-percent-cost-of-living-adjustment-approved-for-fiscal-year-2027.html)

**Not found:** a clean, published per-year early-retirement reduction table (Ohio's is actuarial/table-driven via a member estimator, not a public flat-rate table) — modeling this system precisely will require either that estimator's underlying table or a documented approximation.

## 8. Military (Legacy High-3 and Blended Retirement System) — **[browser-verified]**

`militarypay.defense.gov` and `dfas.mil` — the canonical DoD sources — return a WAF "Access Denied" to both automated fetch and a full browser session; this is a hard access block, not a bot-fetch-only restriction. All facts below were instead verified against the Army's official benefits portal (`myarmybenefits.us.army.mil`), an equally authoritative `.mil` government source, which loads cleanly. The 2.5%/year High-3 formula and the 20-year eligibility cliff were confirmed by direct screenshot of the rendered page (not just extracted text).

- **Legacy High-3 (High-36) formula:** `50% of High-3 average basic pay after 20 years of service, plus an additional 2.5% per additional year`. Verbatim confirmed on-page: "50% +10% (2.5% × four years)" for a 24-year retiree = 60% of High-3 pay. Covers those who entered service (DIEMS) between Sept 8, 1980 and Jul 31, 1986 (an older "Final Pay" plan — same 2.5%/yr but on final base pay, not a 3-year average — covers pre-Sept-8-1980 entrants). [Army: Retired Pay Benefit Fact Sheet](https://myarmybenefits.us.army.mil/Benefit-Library/Federal-Benefits/Retired-Pay?serv=122) — **[browser-verified]**
- **Blended Retirement System (BRS) formula:** `2.0% × years of service × High-3 average basic pay` — a reduced multiplier vs. legacy (20 years = 40%, vs. 50% under High-3). Covers all who entered service on/after Jan 1, 2018 (those already serving could opt in through Dec 31, 2018). [Army: Retired Pay Benefit Fact Sheet](https://myarmybenefits.us.army.mil/Benefit-Library/Federal-Benefits/Retired-Pay?serv=122) · [Army: Blended Retirement System](https://myarmybenefits.us.army.mil/Benefit-Library/Federal-Benefits/Blended-Retirement-System?serv=122)
- **BRS TSP government contribution:** automatic 1% of basic pay starting 60 days after entry, plus up to an additional 4% match on the member's own contributions — **5% total** DoD contribution. Fully vested in the automatic 1% after 2 years of service (the member's own contributions and their matching portion are immediately vested). [Army: Blended Retirement System](https://myarmybenefits.us.army.mil/Benefit-Library/Federal-Benefits/Blended-Retirement-System?serv=122)
- **COLA:** full CPI-linked annual adjustment, effective **December 1st** each year, equal to the % increase between the average 3rd-quarter CPI of the current year vs. the prior year; if CPI decreases, the COLA is floored at zero (never negative), and the next year's COLA "reaches back" to the last 3rd-quarter CPI that produced a positive increase. A retiree's **first** COLA (if retiring between Jan 1 and Sep 31) is a specially prorated "partial COLA," distinct from the standard formula, to prevent double-dipping a pay raise and a full COLA in the same first year. (The older REDUX variant gets CPI minus 1 point when the raw CPI increase exceeds 1%; at 1% or less, REDUX gets the same COLA as every other plan — an edge case not needed if only High-3/BRS are modeled.) [Army: Retired Pay Benefit Fact Sheet](https://myarmybenefits.us.army.mil/Benefit-Library/Federal-Benefits/Retired-Pay?serv=122) · [DoD: Retirement COLA — Wayback-archived, since militarypay.defense.gov blocks live automated access](https://web.archive.org/web/20260411120137/https://militarypay.defense.gov/Pay/Retirement/Cola.aspx) — **[browser-verified]**
- **Survivor Benefit Plan (SBP):** opt-in annuity elected at retirement (declining full spouse coverage requires the spouse's written, notarized consent). Annuity = **55% of the elected base amount** paid to the surviving spouse for life; premium = **6.5% of the elected base amount**, deducted pre-tax from the retiree's own retired pay while paid. Coverage becomes paid-up (premiums stop) after 30 years of premiums AND age 70; the survivor annuity itself receives the annual COLA. Structurally the direct analogue of the WA DRS survivor election already in the codebase — an at-retirement election that reduces the member's own benefit to fund a survivor share — but SBP uses a flat 6.5%-of-base premium and 55%-of-base annuity rather than DRS's age-difference actuarial factor table. [Army: Survivor Benefit Plan (SBP)](https://myarmybenefits.us.army.mil/Benefit-Library/Federal-Benefits/Survivor-Benefit-Plan-(SBP)?serv=122) · [Army: SBP Fact Sheet (PDF)](https://soldierforlife.army.mil/documents/army%20retirement/retirement%20planning/SBP%20Fact%20Sheet_January%202024.pdf)
- **Eligibility — the 20-year cliff (critical, must be modeled explicitly):** **no pension whatsoever below 20 years of service, under EITHER system.** This is a hard cliff, not graded vesting — unlike WA DRS's service-year eligibility guards, which still vest partial benefits. ## Cross-system flags for the data-landing phases (2–3)

1. **Tier/hire-date dependence is severe** for Texas TRS, NYSTRS, and — differently — Ohio STRS (date-scheduled eligibility steps). Any hardcoded factor in `retirementData.js` must be labeled with the tier/date vintage it represents, the same way the existing `US_STATE_TAX`/`INTL_TAX` entries are dated to `TAX_YEAR`.
2. **COLA treatment varies enormously and must NOT be modeled uniformly:** FERS is CPI-banded and starts at 62; CalSTRS is a flat 2% simple (non-compounding); CalPERS is up to 2% compounded; Texas TRS has none; NYSTRS is 50%-of-CPI on a capped base; Ohio STRS is board-set annually (currently 1.6%, history of suspension); military is full CPI-W. Treating these as one constant would misstate every non-WA-DRS system.
3. **Survivor-benefit mechanics split into two families:** WA DRS-style actuarial factor tables (also CalSTRS/CalPERS, though unpublished/per-election) vs. flat-percentage election mechanics (FERS: 10%/5% cost for 50%/25% survivor share; military SBP: 6.5% cost for 55% share). The generic defined-benefit fallback (§1) should expose a simple flat reduction-% input to cover both families without over-fitting to DRS's actuarial-table shape.
4. **Access-blocked sources:** `militarypay.defense.gov`, `dfas.mil`, `nystrs.org` (HTML pages), and `strsoh.org` (all pages) block automated access outright — not just scraping-hostile headers, but a hard WAF/Cloudflare challenge that also blocks a full browser session. The Wayback Machine (`web.archive.org`) reliably recovers a real, dated snapshot of the blocked page for all of these (confirmed working for `militarypay.defense.gov`, `nystrs.org`, and `strsoh.org` in this pass) and should be the first fallback before falling back further to an alternate official domain or a site's search index. A human maintainer refreshing this data annually (per `docs/investment-data-refresh.md`'s pattern) can use the same Wayback approach rather than needing to solve the CAPTCHA/challenge manually — though a fresh capture should be requested (`web.archive.org/save/<url>`) if the cached one predates the refresh window.
5. **FERS carries a Social-Security-shaped feature, not just a pension formula:** the Special Retirement Supplement (§2) bridges MRA+30/60+20 retirees to age 62, is subject to the same earnings test as Social Security (same SSA-set exempt amount, statutory link via 5 U.S.C. §8421a), and should be derived from this app's existing income-based PIA estimate rather than a new from-scratch SS calculation. This is the one system in this brief that touches `src/finance/socialSecurity.js` as well as the pension module.
